NeuroScope 3D
See a brain tumour in 3D, measure it, and know how far to trust it. Health-tech · 3D medical image segmentation + web visualisation · live demo + judge test pack · 24 hours.
Research and teaching prototype only. It must never present model output as a diagnosis, and it uses public, de-identified research data only.
The story
Ishita, a third-year BTech student, volunteers at a radiology teaching lab. Today's teaching case comes from a public research dataset: an adult glioma, scanned four different ways, each scan a stack of 155 slices.
The trainee beside her scrolls up and down for ten minutes, building the tumour in his head. Is it one mass or two? Which side is it on? How big is it, in millilitres?
A model can colour every voxel that looks like tumour. But a coloured blob helps only if it sits in the right place, on the right side, at the right size, and admits when it is unsure.
Build the tool Ishita wants: drop in a scan, watch the suspected tumour appear inside a 3D brain, measure it, and see exactly how much to trust it.
Problem overview
A brain MRI study is four co-registered 3D volumes: T1, T1Gd (T1 after a contrast agent), T2 and FLAIR. Expert labels mark three tissue types: necrotic core (NCR), oedema (ED) and enhancing tumour (ET). Models and metrics use three nested regions built from them:

Whole tumour (WT)
NCR + ED + ET
Tumour core (TC)
NCR + ET
Enhancing tumour (ET)

Build a web platform that takes one study, segments these regions with a 3D model, shows them in 3D and in linked slices, measures their volumes, and reports how accurate the model is and where it may fail.
The challenge
Every submission must do all of the following:
Upload and validate. Accept four NIfTI files (.nii or .nii.gz), one per sequence; the user or the filename says which file is which. Reject, with a clear message, any file that is unreadable, not 3D, not finite, constant (such as all zeros) or larger than 1 GB, and any set whose shapes or affines differ.
Optional expert mask. Accept an optional label file in BraTS 2021 codes (1 NCR, 2 ED, 4 ET). When it is present, show its regions and volumes next to the prediction.
Segment live. Run a pretrained or fine-tuned 3D model on the uploaded case. Produce WT, TC and ET masks mapped back to the original grid and affine. Also provide a command or endpoint that takes the four files and writes the predicted label map as NIfTI, in BraTS 2021 codes (1, 2, 4) on the input grid and affine; the judges' scorer reads it.
3D view. Show the brain surface and each region as meshes in millimetre (world) space. Users can rotate, zoom, toggle each region and change opacity.
Linked slices. Show axial, coronal and sagittal slices with the mask overlay and L/R, A/P and S/I labels. One crosshair links all three views and the 3D view, and its position is shown as voxel indices and world millimetres.
Volume. Report each region's volume in mL as voxel count × voxel volume from the header spacing, with the formula and the numbers shown.
Evaluate. Report Dice and sensitivity per region on at least 10 held-out labelled cases, split by patient. List the split, and disclose any overlap between your evaluation cases and your model's training data.
Fail safely. Handle broken, incomplete and unsupported inputs with clear messages, and warn or refuse when a set is clearly not a skull-stripped brain MRI. Never show a result for an input you could not process.
Report. Generate a report page or PDF with volumes, metrics, provenance (model name, version and licence; preprocessing; input SHA-256; code commit; timestamp), limitations and the disclaimer. The disclaimer also appears on every screen.
Extra credit: colour-coded agreement with the expert mask; uncertainty from test-time augmentation or an ensemble; finer out-of-distribution warnings; a quality warning instead of a rejection when a sequence is missing; HD95 in millimetres; modality-ablation explanations labelled as not clinical evidence; DICOM input; comparing two scans of one patient over time.
Out of scope: diagnosis, grading, genetic prediction, prognosis or treatment advice; surgical or radiotherapy planning claims; real patient scans; clinical-validation or regulatory claims.

Rules
Data: public, de-identified research data only. No real patient scans. Never send scans to third-party services or language-model APIs.
Models: any pretrained model public before the event, with its licence documented. Fine-tune only on the dev subset or the Decathlon set, and list the case IDs. At inference your pipeline may not read any stored dataset images or labels: a lookup disqualifies the team.
Hidden cases: never train on, tune on or keep the cases the judges give you. Delete uploads automatically within 24 hours.
Live pipeline: a pre-computed case is allowed only as a labelled backup.
Compute: one case, from upload to report, within 2 minutes on the organizer GPU (T4 class).
Wording: say "suspected tumour region predicted by the model" and "estimated volume". Never say "tumour detected", "diagnostic system" or "replaces the radiologist".
